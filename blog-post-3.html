How to sound like yourself online

A short exercise for finding the three words your brand should never sound like.

Most brand voice guides start with the words a brand wants to sound like: friendly, bold, trustworthy. Those words are true of almost every business and therefore useless for actually writing anything. We start from the other direction.

List what you're not

In every strategy session, we ask owners to name three ways their brand should never sound — corporate, salesy, try-hard, overly casual, whatever fits. Constraints are far easier to write against than aspirations. "Don't sound corporate" tells you what to cut from a draft in a way "sound friendly" never will.

Read it out loud

If a caption sounds wrong read aloud in your own voice, it'll sound wrong to the person reading it too. This catches most of the generic-sounding drafts we see, because stiff, over-polished copy is usually written to be read silently, not spoken.

Borrow your own best sentence

Think of the last time a customer complimented how you explained something in person. That sentence — the real one, from a real conversation — is usually closer to your actual brand voice than anything a style guide could produce. Keep a running note of these and mine them for captions.

Consistency beats personality

A brand voice doesn't need to be quirky to be memorable. It needs to sound like the same person wrote every post. That's the bar we coach clients toward — not "more personality," just "the same one, every time."
