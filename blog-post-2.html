The content calendar template we hand every client

A simple structure that survives busy weeks, borrowed straight from our coaching sessions.

Every client gets the same starting calendar, then customises it. Not because businesses are all the same, but because most content calendars fail for the same reason: they're too detailed to survive a busy Tuesday.

Three columns, not thirty

Our starting template has three columns: pillar, format, and purpose. Pillar is one of your three content themes. Format is the shape it takes — a short video, a carousel, a single photo with caption. Purpose is the one-line answer to what it's doing for the reader. That's it. No hashtag banks, no caption drafts, no colour-coded moods — those come later, once the habit is built.

Plan in weeks, not months

A month of content planned in advance sounds responsible, but it usually gets abandoned by week two because life happened and nothing was flexible. We plan one week at a time, with a fifteen-minute Sunday session to fill in the three columns for the week ahead. It's small enough to actually happen.

Leave room for the unplanned post

The calendar always has one open slot per week with no assigned pillar. That's for whatever's actually happening in the business that week — a genuine behind-the-scenes moment, a customer question worth answering publicly, a reaction to something timely. Planned content builds the foundation; unplanned content is usually what people remember.

Review monthly, not daily

Checking metrics every day makes you reactive to noise. Once a month, look back at which pillar and format combinations performed best, and let that — not a single viral post — adjust next month's plan.
